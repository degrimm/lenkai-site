The rescue centre

When a girl runs, she has to have somewhere to run to.

Lenkai takes in girls escaping female genital mutilation and forced childhood marriage, and keeps them in school until they finish. It is why a school that opened with five pupils now boards children.

What we do

Girls arrive here from across Kajiado County, usually with nothing. Some come with a chief or a children’s officer, some come with a relative, some come alone. We give them a bed, food, a uniform and a classroom, and we do not send them back into danger.

What happens next

In earlier years they finished primary at Lenkai and were placed in girls’ schools across Kenya, and most of the girls rescued then went on to secondary school. Today the school runs Junior and Senior School itself, so a girl can stay from the day she arrives until the day she finishes. Where reconciliation with family is safe, we work towards it. Where it is not, she stays.

Hope Beyond Foundation

The school works alongside the Hope Beyond Foundation, founded by Rev. John Parit, which has rescued more than 500 girls from FGM and childhood marriage in Kajiado County alone. The Foundation handles rescue and reintegration; the school handles education.

What a girl receives

  • A bed in the boarding wing
  • Meals from the school kitchen
  • A uniform, so she looks like every other pupil
  • A place in the class that fits her
  • A school to finish in, through Senior School

The path through

From the gate to finishing school

She arrives

Referred by a chief, a children’s officer, a church, a relative, or she walks in. Nobody is turned back into a forced marriage.

She is safe and fed

A place in the boarding wing under the Rescue & Boarding Head, meals from the school kitchen, and a uniform so she looks like every other pupil.

She is placed in a class

Assessed and put where she actually is, not where her age says she should be. Many arrive years behind and catch up fast.

She sits every national assessment

KPSEA at Grade 6, KJSEA at Grade 9, at Lenkai, alongside everyone else.

She stays, all the way through

Lenkai now runs Junior and Senior School on the same campus, so a rescued girl no longer has to leave the place that took her in. Sponsorship is what carries her through these final years, and it is where support most often runs out.

Safeguarding

Why you will not see these girls’ faces

Publishing a rescued girl’s face and story on the open internet can put her at risk from the family she escaped. This site names no child, and publishes no identifying image without written consent from a guardian or guardian‑equivalent.

Support this work

Sponsorship keeps a rescued girl in a classroom rather than back in a homestead. It is the most direct thing an outsider can do.
